# Source Policy

## Approved Official Destinations

Use an official product domain, official documentation, official GitHub organization/repository, official store listing, official VS Code/JetBrains marketplace listing, or official package-manager documentation when it is the real installation path.

Verify that a regional store URL, language selector, publisher identity, and availability match the target market. A listing visible in one country is not proof that it is available in another.

## Avoid

- APK portals, download aggregators, and Softonic-style sites;
- reuploaded forks, portable bundles, and unknown installers;
- cracked, unlocked, premium-free, modded, or patched builds;
- SEO wrappers or pages that imitate an official app;
- secondary pages as destinations when an official route exists.

Use secondary sources only to understand user confusion or SERP quality; never present them as an official download channel.

## No-Installer Rule

If the product is web-only, extension-only, store-only, GitHub-only, or documentation-led, name that route explicitly. Do not use “download” language that suggests a nonexistent installer. Repeat the clarification in limitations, editorial summary, an editorial section, FAQ, and safety notes.

## Evidence Discipline

Keep source URL, access date, market, and claim supported. Verify time-sensitive facts such as pricing, availability, privacy commitments, legal claims, and platform support from primary sources near publication time. Mark unresolved regional uncertainty instead of guessing.
